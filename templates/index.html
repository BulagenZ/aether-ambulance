    A* search

    Lecture 2 · Informed search

    Job here: find the fastest route to the hospital, and later the detour.

    How it works: it keeps a priority queue of cells and always expands the one with the lowest f = g + h. g is minutes driven so far; h is the Manhattan distance to the hospital.

    Why it fits: Manhattan distance never overestimates on a four-direction grid, so the route is guaranteed shortest while fewer cells are expanded than with BFS.

    f(n) = g(n) + h(n)
    Code: astar.py
    2

    HMM · Viterbi

    Lecture 7 · Hidden Markov Models

    Job here: work out whether traffic on the risky road is Flowing or Jammed from three speed reports.

    How it works: for every time step and hidden state it stores the probability of the best state sequence ending there, then backtracks from the best final state.

    Why it fits: the traffic state cannot be seen directly, only noisy reports, and it tends to persist. That is a hidden Markov model.

    Vt(s) = maxp Vt−1(p) · T(p→s) · E(s, ot)
    Code: viterbi.py
    3

    Bayesian inference

    Lecture 6 · Probability & Bayes

    Job here: update the chance that the road is blocked after one sensor reading.

    How it works: multiply the prior by how likely the reading is in each case, then normalise.

    Why it fits: the sensor gives false alarms and misses, so one reading should shift the belief, not replace it.

    P(Blocked | obs) = P(obs | Blocked) · P(Blocked) / P(obs)
    Code: bayes.py
    4

    Markov decision process

    MDP & RL lecture

    Job here: choose between Continue and Reroute.

    How it works: states are Clear and Blocked, reward is minus minutes. One Bellman update gives Q(s, a); the Q-values are then weighted by the belief from Bayes and the higher one wins.

    Why it fits: driving on has an uncertain outcome, so the agent must compare expected values rather than best cases.

    Q(s,a) = Σs′ T(s′|s,a) · [ R(a,s′) + γ·V(s′) ]
    Code: mdp.py. V starts at 0, so γ has no effect in this one-step version.

    The real-world problem

    An ambulance usually takes the shortest road. If that road turns out to be blocked, the crew loses time driving in, turning round and taking the long way. Taking the long way every time is safe but slow.

    The right choice depends on how likely the blockage is, and that is uncertain: traffic reports are indirect and sensors make mistakes.

    What AETHER does

    It plans a route, estimates the chance the shortest road is blocked from noisy evidence, and decides whether to drive on or reroute before the ambulance commits.

    What it is not

    • It does not use real-time or historical traffic data.
    • It is not connected to any emergency dispatch service.
    • It must not be used to make real routing decisions.

    Course project

    Final project for AI Fundamentals. It combines four algorithms from four course lectures (search, Bayesian reasoning, hidden Markov models and MDPs), with A* used twice, in one chained pipeline.

    How it is built

    • Backend: Python and Flask. The algorithms live in astar.py, viterbi.py, bayes.py and mdp.py.
    • Frontend: HTML, CSS and JavaScript. It only draws what the backend returns.
    • Tests: python -m unittest -v checks each algorithm, the three scenarios and the web API.

    Limitations and next steps

    • Only one road is uncertain. A real city needs a belief for every road.
    • The probabilities are invented. They could be learned from data.
    • The MDP looks one step ahead. Value iteration over the whole trip would let the agent re-decide at every cell.
    • The rush-hour decision is close (one minute), so it is sensitive to the assumed numbers.
